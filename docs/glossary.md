# Glossary

These concise definitions aid cross-chapter reading; they do not replace the full definitions or their conditions. Chapter references indicate where a term is developed. A statement in the Boundary column is a limit on interpretation, not an additional empirical finding.

| Term                                                | Reader definition                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Chapter          | Boundary                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
|-----------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 3M Taxonomy                                         | Chapter 9 descriptive taxonomy of Manifestness, Modality, and Materiality used around the empirical participation apparatus.                                                                                                                     | Ch. 9            | Only Act Form (the reader-facing Modality field) and RO/FP/AD were prospectively frozen; the full 3M instrument was not prospectively validated.                                                                                |
| Absorption Capacity                                 | A bounded domain/actor condition limiting how much of a candidate trajectory can be effectively assimilated or realized.                                                                                                                         | Ch. 6            | A subtype/example of realization conditions, not the meaning of $Z_{d}(t)$ as a whole.                                                                                                                                          |
| Act Form                                            | Chapter 9 empirical act-category field carried forward from the frozen Modality field.                                                                                                                                                           | Ch. 9            | Chapter 9 uses Act Form for its empirical Modality field; Chapter 8’s descriptive label is Visible Form.                                                                                                                        |
| Actor Dependence (AD)                               | The degree to which the significance of an act depends on who performs it or the role/institution through which it is performed.                                                                                                                 | Ch. 8            | AD does not establish prestige, correctness, or moral standing.                                                                                                                                                                 |
| AD                                                  | See Actor Dependence (AD).                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | Ch. 8            | Relational Grammar abbreviation.                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| Adaptive Disequilibrium                             | The provisional stronger claim of continued adaptation rather than settlement into an absorbing equilibrium.                                                                                                                                     | Chs. 2 & 12      | Within a declared horizon, specified disturbances that no longer alter the realized state would narrow or defeat the horizon-bounded claim; the general form is orienting.                                                      |
| AGI                                                 | Artificial intelligence whose capability profile matches or exceeds the best unaugmented human capability across declared human capability domains; empirical comparisons declare domains, assistance policy, baseline, and coverage criterion.  | Ch. 1            | See Definition 0 and Chapter 1 §1.3 for the stipulative definition and operational scope. Comparisons declare the system boundary; no universal binary arrival test is supplied.                                                |
| AGI-Inclusive Humanity                              | The evolving human–AI configuration within which artificial capability acquires institutional and social consequence, and within which realization is evaluated with a human-centered normative orientation.                                     | Ch. 2            | Not a terminal destination; artificial moral status remains bracketed.                                                                                                                                                          |
| Autonomous Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI)         | Repeated AI-dependent improvement cycles in which AI systems materially contribute to improving later AI capability and humans no longer supply the decisive technical decisions at every step.                                                  | Ch. 11           | Not demonstrated by the current book evidence; capability feedback and rapid progress are insufficient.                                                                                                                         |
| Availability                                        | The degree to which capability is accessible or deployable in the relevant setting.                                                                                                                                                              | Ch. 4            | Availability ≠ Reach; indirect exposure can exist without direct access.                                                                                                                                                        |
| Benefit                                             | An indexed comparative evaluation of realized consequences for the human beneficiaries within AGI-inclusive humanity.                                                                                                                            | Ch. 2            | Requires a baseline or feasible alternative, beneficiary scope, and evaluative criteria; not an argument of the realization mapping.                                                                                            |
| Bottleneck Relocation                               | Relaxation of one constraint that makes another relatively more important while the relevant opportunity set remains substantially intact.                                                                                                       | Ch. 4            | Distinct from Path Foreclosure, which changes the opportunity set itself.                                                                                                                                                       |
| Candidate Trajectory                                | A possible developmental path Γ evaluated as an object that includes transitions, intermediate states, and opportunity-set effects, not merely its endpoint.                                                                                     | Ch. 6            | Endpoint similarity does not imply trajectory equivalence.                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Ceiling                                             | A domain-scoped capability readout used inside CFS.                                                                                                                                                                                              | Ch. 5            | Ceiling ≠ Potential P(t).                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Ceiling–Floor–Slope (CFS)                           | A bounded submodel for analyzing the dynamics of a capability–realization gap in a specified domain.                                                                                                                                             | Ch. 5            | CFS is not the general realization model.                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Claim Status                                        | The evidentiary status assigned to a claim after considering observation, representation, inference, limitations, and versioning.                                                                                                                | Ch. 10           | Claim status is version- and evidence-conditioned.                                                                                                                                                                              |
| Conditional Non-Fungibility                         | Proposition A: similar visible act forms need not be analytically interchangeable when their RO/FP/AD profiles differ under declared conditions.                                                                                                 | Chs. 8–9         | Proposition A’s exact evidentiary status appears once in the Claims and Status Register below.                                                                                                                                  |
| Definition 0                                        | The book’s stipulative AGI definition: a capability profile matching or exceeding the unaugmented human envelope across declared human capability domains.                                                                                       | Ch. 1            | This entry is a scope gloss, not a replacement of Definition 0’s canonical wording. Application requires declared comparison coordinates, system boundary, assistance policy, baseline, and coverage; not one universal scalar. |
| Direction Without Terminality                       | Orientation toward a purpose does not by itself establish a terminal state.                                                                                                                                                                      | Chs. 2 & 12      | High-confidence candidate; neither assumes nor rules out terminal closure.                                                                                                                                                      |
| Domain-Specific Realization Conditions              | The domain-indexed conditions represented by $Z_{d}(t)$ that shape whether and how Potential becomes Realization.                                                                                                                                | Ch. 4            | Not reducible to absorption capacity alone; no universal exhaustive vector is assumed.                                                                                                                                          |
| Epistemic Discipline                                | The book’s rules for separating what exists, what is observed, how it is represented, what is inferred, and what claim status is justified.                                                                                                      | Ch. 10           | Not a new argument of the realization mapping.                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Epistemic Repairability                             | The capacity to detect error, localize failure, identify dependencies, revise affected structure, propagate consequences, and expose the revised object to renewed scrutiny.                                                                     | Chs. 3 & 10      | Repair preserves the failed version and its result; revised objects do not automatically inherit validation.                                                                                                                    |
| Exposure                                            | A group-specific state of Reach, Susceptibility, and Recourse through which realization is encountered.                                                                                                                                          | Chs. 4 & 7       | Exposure is an argument of realization, not a fifth governance point.                                                                                                                                                           |
| Facet                                               | A metadata or analytical property of a theoretical record that plateau membership cannot reconstruct without relevant information loss.                                                                                                          | Ch. 3            | Cross-cutting classification; independence from plateau role is functional, not a claim of statistical independence.                                                                                                            |
| Feedback                                            | A relation in which an earlier realized outcome, observation, representation, or response alters conditions relevant to subsequent realization.                                                                                                  | Ch. 11           | Sequence alone is not enough; a return mechanism must be documented.                                                                                                                                                            |
| Floor                                               | A specified realized or assimilated state used inside CFS.                                                                                                                                                                                       | Ch. 5            | Floor ≠ general Realization $R_{g}(t)$.                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Foreclosure Position (FP)                           | Whether meaningful alternative realization options remain open or are being narrowed/closed relative to the act.                                                                                                                                 | Ch. 8            | FP is not identical to veto authority.                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| FP                                                  | See Foreclosure Position (FP).                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Ch. 8            | Relational Grammar abbreviation.                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| Frame One                                           | Chapter 9’s frozen empirical frame of 49 projects used to test the Participation propositions and related empirical distinctions.                                                                                                                | Ch. 9            | Post-freeze evidence and cases outside the frozen frame cannot change N, historical coding, proposition verdicts, reliability claims, headline rates, or Responsive Floor adjudication.                                         |
| Governance                                          | Institutional processes that execute, observe, steer, and procedurally adjudicate contested realization pathways.                                                                                                                                | Ch. 7            | Governance surrounds/modifies realization; it is not another universal input to $\mu_{t}$.                                                                                                                                      |
| Governance Functions                                | Execution, Observation, Steering, and Procedural Adjudication—the functional roles governance performs.                                                                                                                                          | Ch. 7            | Functions ≠ points of purchase.                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Governance Points of Purchase                       | Access, Sequencing, Distribution, and Deployment Context—the locations where governance can intervene in a realization pathway.                                                                                                                  | Ch. 7            | Points of purchase answer where governance intervenes, not what function it performs.                                                                                                                                           |
| Governance Propositions (GP1–GP7)                   | The seven governance propositions assessed individually in Chapter 7.                                                                                                                                                                            | Ch. 7, Table 7.1 | Their unequal statuses are retained; this label does not imply collective validation.                                                                                                                                           |
| Gradual AGI                                         | The book’s inquiry into AGI-related transformation through heterogeneous realization, distinguishing phenomenal gradualness from principled graduality.                                                                                          | Chs. 1–3         | Gradualness does not require slow, smooth, or continuous change; the descriptive criterion does not entail a normative principle.                                                                                               |
| Indexed Optimality                                  | A claim of optimality indexed to specified objectives, constraints, metrics, actors or evaluators, time horizon, uncertainty assumptions, and scope.                                                                                             | Ch. 6            | There is no free-standing or civilization-scale optimum unless a common ordering has actually been established.                                                                                                                 |
| Individuation Rule                                  | The advance specification of the transformation being classified and what observation would exhaust it.                                                                                                                                          | Ch. 1            | Fixes the scope of a phenomenal-gradualness claim and permits that claim to fail.                                                                                                                                               |
| Inference                                           | The conclusion drawn from observation and representation.                                                                                                                                                                                        | Ch. 10           | Inference ≠ Claim Status.                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Local Scalarity / Global Non-Scalarity              | Bounded evaluators may use local scalar objectives without establishing a canonical universal scalar for the whole system.                                                                                                                       | Ch. 12, §12.6.2  | High-confidence candidate; admission as canonical is not yet complete.                                                                                                                                                          |
| Manifestness                                        | A 3M descriptive dimension: whether a participatory act overtly presents itself as addressing a realization (Explicit) or relates to it through behavior, practice, or contribution (Implicit); not how readily evidence of it can be recovered. | Ch. 9            | Secondary descriptive lens in Frame One, not a frozen Proposition A discriminant field.                                                                                                                                         |
| Materiality                                         | A 3M descriptive dimension: how a participatory act is instantiated, as Intangible, Tangible, or Mixed (Chapter 9 §9.2.1).                                                                                                                       | Ch. 9            | Concerns the act’s instantiation, not the magnitude of its consequence, and is not assigned retrospectively from success. Distinct from Chapter 8’s material-difference threshold and from the §9.1.1 relevance rule.           |
| Modality                                            | The 3M dimension corresponding to the type/form of participatory act; operationalized prospectively through the frozen Act Form field.                                                                                                           | Ch. 9            | Chapter 8’s descriptive label is Visible Form.                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Multi-Plateau Framework (MPF)                       | A logical architecture organizing kinds of theoretical work—definition, formalization, empirical contact, synthesis—without treating them as chronological stages.                                                                               | Ch. 3            | Plateaus are not a universal societal sequence or hierarchy.                                                                                                                                                                    |
| Nash Bargaining Solution (NBS)                      | A bounded selection rule applicable only when bargaining assumptions, utilities, and disagreement points are defensible.                                                                                                                         | Ch. 6            | If assumptions are not defensible: ‘NBS not presently applicable.’ Distinct from the Nash equilibrium discussed in Ch. 7, §7.3.10.                                                                                              |
| Nash Equilibrium                                    | A strategy profile in a specified game at which no participant can improve its payoff by changing its strategy alone, holding the others’ strategies fixed.                                                                                      | Ch. 7, §7.3.10   | Distinct from Chapter 6’s Nash Bargaining Solution; existence or stability does not establish safety, and necessity for trustworthiness is not established.                                                                     |
| NotAdjudicated                                      | A formal empirical status used when available evidence does not permit the relevant classification to be decided under the specified design.                                                                                                     | Chs. 9 & 12      | NotAdjudicated ≠ unsupported, untested, unexercised, or measurement-limited; it does not mean NotSupported.                                                                                                                     |
| Observation                                         | The evidence or measurement obtained about an underlying state.                                                                                                                                                                                  | Ch. 10           | Observation ≠ Representation or Inference.                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Observation Opportunity                             | The opportunity an evidence-generating process provides to observe a phenomenon if it is present.                                                                                                                                                | Ch. 10           | Non-observation supports absence only to the extent that detection opportunity was adequate.                                                                                                                                    |
| Ongoing, Unfinished Transformation                  | An orientation for theory-building; diagnostically, continuing change to later realization conditions and absence of established terminal closure for a specified domain and horizon.                                                            | Chs. 3 & 11      | Chapter 3 provides orientation; Chapter 11 gives process/closure diagnostics. The orientation and diagnostic have distinct roles.                                                                                               |
| Optimization                                        | Evaluation and, where justified, selection among candidate trajectories under specified objectives, constraints, horizons, uncertainty assumptions, and scope.                                                                                   | Ch. 6            | Optimization is upstream of realization and is not a fifth argument of $\mu_{t}$.                                                                                                                                               |
| Optimization Subject                                | The bounded system or object whose developmental trajectory is being evaluated in Chapter 6.                                                                                                                                                     | Ch. 6            | Optimization Subject ≠ actor ≠ objective.                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Pareto Efficiency                                   | A nondominance filter used to exclude trajectories that can be improved for at least one evaluated party without worsening another.                                                                                                              | Ch. 6            | Structural filter, not a complete social-choice rule.                                                                                                                                                                           |
| Participation                                       | A reciprocal human–AI process in which actors can shape the conditions, uses, verification, values, and consequences of realization.                                                                                                             | Ch. 8            | Reciprocity does not imply symmetry, equal agency, equal standing, or equal responsibility.                                                                                                                                     |
| Participation-sensitive Floor                       | Property of a societal Floor: features of human Participatory Configuration contribute to differences in its realization path or configuration, whether or not a study can establish it.                                                         | Ch. 8            | A property, not a classification; Responsive Floor is the evidence-conditioned classification.                                                                                                                                  |
| Participation-Sensitive Realization (Proposition C) | The participation claim examined through bounded pathways from acts and consequences to realization-relevant change.                                                                                                                             | Ch. 9, §9.6      | Documented non-veto association sequences establish neither prevalence nor an identified causal effect.                                                                                                                         |
| Participatory Act                                   | An act by a Participatory Subject that may enter recognition, uptake, consequence, and possible realization pathways.                                                                                                                            | Ch. 8            | Act does not entail Consequence or Realization change.                                                                                                                                                                          |
| Participatory Consequence                           | A consequence attributable at the participation layer before any further inference to realization change.                                                                                                                                        | Ch. 8            | Participatory Consequence ≠ general realized consequence.                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Participatory Readiness                             | Capacity to recognize a realization-relevant issue, form a position or claim, and undertake an intentional Participatory Act.                                                                                                                    | Chs. 8–9         | Distinct from standing, willingness, temporal opportunity, and an act actually performed.                                                                                                                                       |
| Participatory Role Plasticity                       | Proposition B concept describing redistribution or migration of functions between human and AI actors within shared processes.                                                                                                                   | Ch. 8            | Untested in Chapter 9 Frame One; prospective target.                                                                                                                                                                            |
| Participatory Subject                               | The subject whose participation is analytically represented in the Chapter 8 human-side apparatus.                                                                                                                                               | Ch. 8            | Subject ≠ Actor ≠ Instrument.                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Path Foreclosure                                    | Narrowing or closure of substantively different future realization options through commitments, constraints, or prior choices.                                                                                                                   | Chs. 4, 6 & 8    | Foreclosure Position locates an act relative to the remaining alternatives; foreclosure is not identical to veto authority.                                                                                                     |
| Phenomenal Gradualness                              | Given an individuation rule fixing the transformation under study, a specified AGI-related transformation is gradual when its relevant realization is not exhausted by one event.                                                                | Ch. 1            | Phenomenal concerns the form of transformation, not subjective experience.                                                                                                                                                      |
| Potential                                           | Capability or possibility that may be realizable under specified conditions.                                                                                                                                                                     | Ch. 4            | Potential ≠ Realization.                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Potential–Realization Separation                    | What a system can do is distinct from what becomes instantiated for specified groups under actual conditions.                                                                                                                                    | Chs. 1–4 & 12    | Capability does not entail realization, and realization does not entail benefit.                                                                                                                                                |
| Principled Graduality                               | Information-responsive iteration combined with preserved revisability.                                                                                                                                                                           | Ch. 1            | Not entailed by phenomenal gradualness; revisability does not guarantee reversible outcomes.                                                                                                                                    |
| Procedural Settlement ≠ Substantive Settlement      | Institutional closure of procedure, authority, or entitlement can leave contested ultimate ends unresolved.                                                                                                                                      | Ch. 12, §12.6.2  | High-confidence candidate; procedural closure does not establish substantive agreement.                                                                                                                                         |
| Reach                                               | Existence and extent of direct or mediated contact with an AI-mediated capability or consequence.                                                                                                                                                | Ch. 4            | Weak Reach means limited contact, not necessarily low consequence conditional on contact.                                                                                                                                       |
| Realization                                         | The realized state for an indexed group at a specified time under heterogeneous conditions represented by the Chapter 4 mapping.                                                                                                                 | Ch. 4            | Realization ≠ benefit; realization is not a universal scalar.                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Realization Orientation (RO)                        | How a participatory act is oriented relative to a realization trajectory—for example toward enabling, redirecting, pausing, or otherwise shaping it.                                                                                             | Ch. 8            | RO is relational, not a simple positive/negative sentiment score.                                                                                                                                                               |
| Realization Processes Are Reflexive                 | Realization can alter conditions relevant to later realization.                                                                                                                                                                                  | Ch. 12, §12.6.2  | High-confidence candidate; temporal succession alone does not establish such alteration.                                                                                                                                        |
| Realized Consequence                                | A change or effect that may follow from an instantiated realized state.                                                                                                                                                                          | Ch. 2            | Distinct from realization itself and from the evaluation of benefit.                                                                                                                                                            |
| Recognition                                         | An intermediate state in which an act is noticed or registered by a relevant actor or institution.                                                                                                                                               | Ch. 8            | Recognition ≠ Uptake and does not itself imply consequence.                                                                                                                                                                     |
| Recourse                                            | Practical capacity to recognize, contest, redirect, correct, exit, or obtain remedy for an AI-mediated decision or consequence.                                                                                                                  | Chs. 4 & 7       | Nominal appeal channels are not automatically effective Recourse.                                                                                                                                                               |
| Recursion                                           | Repeated return of changed conditions through successive realization cycles.                                                                                                                                                                     | Ch. 11           | Recursion ≠ Autonomous Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI).                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Recursion Without Ontological Proliferation         | Return paths can change existing objects and relations without requiring a new fundamental category for each feedback product.                                                                                                                   | Ch. 12, §12.6.2  | High-confidence candidate; feedback is not a fifth argument of the realization mapping.                                                                                                                                         |
| Recursive Re-entry                                  | A changed condition produced by an earlier stage enters a later realization cycle.                                                                                                                                                               | Ch. 11           | Recursive re-entry is weaker than repeated recursion.                                                                                                                                                                           |
| Recursive Self-Improvement                          | An AI system materially contributes to improving its own capabilities, or those of successor systems, and the improved capability contributes to another improvement cycle. RSI can be graded and can remain human-directed (§11.2).             | Ch. 11           | See Autonomous Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) for the narrower case, which adds that human direction no longer supplies the key technical decisions at every step.                                                            |
| Reflexivity                                         | A condition in which representations, claims, or responses can themselves become part of the environment they describe and alter later behavior or realization.                                                                                  | Ch. 11           | Not every feedback loop is reflexive in the same sense.                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Relational Grammar                                  | The RO–FP–AD grammar used to distinguish participatory acts by realization orientation, foreclosure position, and actor dependence.                                                                                                              | Ch. 8            | Canonical reading order: RO → FP → AD.                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| Representation                                      | The formal, textual, benchmark, dataset, model, or other form in which observations are encoded.                                                                                                                                                 | Ch. 10           | Representation ≠ the underlying state itself.                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Responsive Floor                                    | An evidence-conditioned domain-level classification concerning whether participation can reach realization through documented pathways under specified conditions.                                                                               | Chs. 8–9         | Not democracy, welfare, or a universal participation score; aggregate Chapter 9 status is NotAdjudicated.                                                                                                                       |
| RO                                                  | See Realization Orientation (RO).                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Ch. 8            | Relational Grammar abbreviation.                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| Shared Stewardship                                  | Distributed responsibility for choices affecting a shared developmental subject.                                                                                                                                                                 | Ch. 6            | Does not establish consensus, legitimacy, or authority to decide for affected parties.                                                                                                                                          |
| Slope                                               | Observed synchronization rate: the fraction of the current CFS gap closed during one observation period.                                                                                                                                         | Ch. 5            | Slope is an observable. Under the baseline one-period recurrence, $S_{t} = \beta$ algebraically; this equality does not independently identify or validate structural coupling. Slope is undefined at zero gap.                 |
| Specialized Critic Model                            | A dedicated or specially trained model assigned to identify specified unsafe, insecure, unreliable, or insufficiently supported behavior in another system.                                                                                      | Ch. 7, §7.3.10   | Proposed role within accountable contestation; specialization establishes neither independence nor validated detection, and the governance arrangement is untested here.                                                        |
| Standing                                            | A recognized entitlement to bring a claim into a governance process; concerns who may judge, represent, contest, or authorize claims.                                                                                                            | Chs. 2 & 7       | Standing is distinct from affectedness, participation, and decision authority.                                                                                                                                                  |
| Structural Feedback                                 | Higher-burden feedback in which the realization relationship itself appears to change, not merely the values of its arguments.                                                                                                                   | Ch. 11           | Requires separate evidence; do not infer from ordinary changes in P, Av, Ex, or Z.                                                                                                                                              |
| Susceptibility                                      | Conditional significance of contact once Reach exists: how a group is positioned to experience benefit, burden, displacement, dependency, error, or other consequences.                                                                          | Ch. 4            | Equal Reach does not imply equal Susceptibility; Chapter 7 names and symbolizes it without measuring or calibrating it.                                                                                                         |
| Synchronization                                     | The process by which a bounded capability–realization gap evolves over time under CFS applicability conditions.                                                                                                                                  | Ch. 5            | Not every institutional lag is a CFS gap.                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Synthetic Principle Admission Test (SPAT)           | Five admission checks: source anchoring; recurrence or constituent sufficiency; non-redundancy/discriminability; status inheritance; and a stated defeat condition or scope boundary.                                                            | Ch. 12, §12.6.2  | Incomplete displayed treatment does not warrant promotion to canonical status.                                                                                                                                                  |
| Typed Relation                                      | A connection between theoretical objects whose specified type records how they depend on or bear on one another.                                                                                                                                 | Ch. 3            | Connection does not erase differences between objects; relation types and dependencies remain revisable.                                                                                                                        |
| Underlying State                                    | The state or condition that exists independently of whether it has been successfully observed or represented.                                                                                                                                    | Ch. 10           | Underlying State ≠ Observation.                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Unified Gradual AGI Model                           | The book-level architecture integrating gradualness and graduality, realization, bounded dynamics, Optimization, governance, Participation, epistemic discipline, and feedback while preserving analytical boundaries.                           | Ch. 12           | Unification is architectural, not algebraic; no new master score or fifth argument is introduced.                                                                                                                               |
| Uptake                                              | An intermediate state in which a recognized act is taken up in a relevant process, decision, response, or pathway.                                                                                                                               | Ch. 8            | Uptake ≠ realized effect.                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Visible Form                                        | Chapter 8’s ordinary descriptive label for how a Participatory Act appears or is observed before the Chapter 9 empirical apparatus is introduced.                                                                                                | Ch. 8            | Chapter 8 uses Visible Form; Chapter 9 uses Act Form for its empirical Modality field.                                                                                                                                          |
